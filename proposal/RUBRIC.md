# Grading rubric — Project proposal

**10 points, graded for completion.** The proposal is a one-page plan, due end
of Week 5. It exists so the instructor can catch scope and data problems early,
not to judge the idea. A complete, good-faith proposal is 10/10.

| # | Item | Pts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project title | 1 |
| 2 | Summary: what is the problem and why does it matter | 2 |
| 3 | Data: what dataset(s), where they come from, and (if unlabeled) how labels will be obtained | 2 |
| 4 | Machine learning: which model(s) you expect to try, and a baseline | 2 |
| 5 | Evaluation: metrics and split strategy | 2 |
| 6 | Learning objective: what you expect to learn | 1 |

## Requirements

- **Team roster.** List every team member by name. Teams of up to three;
  groups of three must delineate who does what.
- **Format.** One page, PDF or markdown, committed to your course repo
  (any sensible path/filename — `proposal.md`, `project-proposal.pdf`, etc.).
- **One proposal per team is fine.** Every member should have a copy (or a
  pointer to it) in their own repo so it is found when submissions are pulled.
- **Feedback, not deductions.** Vague data or evaluation sections get a
  comment and a request to firm them up, not a lower score, unless the section
  is missing entirely.
